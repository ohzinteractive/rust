Spores emerge beneath the fronds and drift to new ground.

Golden spores are enlarged for visibility. Each settled spore waits a different amount of time before sprouting. Tiny coiled shoots emerge one at a time from the crown, grow upright, unroll, then spread their leaflets. Opening fronds gradually lower the older leaves. Crowns keep adding and replacing fronds; old leaves brown and droop individually. On the ground, dead ferns remain as litter and gradually raise the soil. New ferns establish where windborne spores land; settled spores can wait for room when the population is full. Only mature crowns with fertile, fully developed fronds release spores. Growth is accelerated and assumes moist ground and successful fertilization after the gametophyte stage. Set wind above zero to carry spores farther.
